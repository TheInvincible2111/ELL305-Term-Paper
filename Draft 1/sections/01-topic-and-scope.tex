\section{Chosen Topic and Present Scope}
\label{sec:scope}

The proposed term paper studies how modern processor design is moving beyond the traditional assumption that most major processor resources must be integrated on a single large monolithic die. The principal case study will be AMD's Zen family of processors, with particular emphasis on two developments: chiplet-based processor organization and three-dimensional stacked last-level cache through AMD 3D V-Cache technology.

The present working research question is:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{How do chiplet-based organization and 3D-stacked cache help modern processors address limitations in performance, scalability, memory latency, power, and die-level integration, and what new trade-offs do these techniques introduce?}
\end{quote}

The intention is not to write a product-history comparison of AMD and Intel. Instead, AMD Zen will be used as a modern case study through which processor-organization and memory-system concepts from ELL305 can be investigated in greater depth. A limited comparison with contemporary Intel approaches may later be included where it helps clarify alternative architectural choices.

The topic has also been selected with the 150-page final-paper requirement in mind. A report restricted to one particular processor or benchmark would probably be too narrow for such a length. The broader formulation permits the final paper to develop the theoretical background -- processor organization, performance, cache hierarchy, multicore systems, interconnects, power, and scaling -- before studying their application in modern chiplet and 3D-cache designs.
