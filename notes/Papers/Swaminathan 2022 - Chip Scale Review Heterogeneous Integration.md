# Bibliographic record

Chip Scale Review, May–June 2022, pp. 11–21. No DOI.

# Relevance

This is the article Ishan's notes call 'Barriers to Large On-Die Caches' — that phrase is a section of this article, not its title. Primary AMD source for why planar L3 expansion became difficult (Part IV) and for first-generation V-Cache component details.

# Reading status

Added to Zotero (collection *ELL305 Term Paper*) on 28 October 2026. Printed page 16 was read via the magazine's HTML flipbook on 28 Oct 2026. It states, as AMD's position: analog circuits have not scaled much and SRAM is not scaling as well as logic; growing on-die cache increases die size and lowers yield, becoming cost-prohibitive and hurting product flexibility; larger area lengthens data paths, raising cache access latency and power; side-by-side cache chiplets improve yield but stacking adds capacity 'without the added lateral distance'. Also: Zen 3 CCD is TSMC 7 nm, 8 cores sharing 32 MB L3, 81 mm²; the L3D die is 41 mm², sized to sit over the CCD's L2 and L3 region; V-Cache support was planned into Zen 3 from the start. Remaining pages not yet read. Corporate author: attribute claims to AMD.
